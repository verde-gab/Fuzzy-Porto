4. Weight assignment

    The second phase of the FIS, consists in assigning a weight to each environmental parametre using a priority analysis, based on an Analytic
Hierarchy Process (AHP). Each city has a different consideration about which pollutant is the most problematic since the topography, pollution
sources and environmental obstacles are different in each place. In this work, the Mexico City Valley has a characteristic geography (see
Section 2) that directly inﬂuences the air quality status. Therefore, the proposed Fuzzy Inference System considers the interactions between the
primary and secondary pollutants based on their different levels of concentration, time to detect and control of secondary pollutant effects. On the
other hand, as seen in Table 1, each pollutant affects human health as well as the greenhouse effect, acid rain and global warming. According to
this, it is necessary to establish an importance level to each parametre based on its negative effect in the ecosystem. For this reason, pollutants
were grouped into three categories according to an importance order:

1. Direct damage to health: this category considered particle matter smaller than 2.5 and 10 μm (PM2.5 and PM10). Although carbon monoxide (CO) is
   a dangerous element, it was excluded from this group because in the Mexico City Valley it rarely exceeds high concentration levels.
2. Direct damage to living tissues: only ozone (O3) was considered in this category because it is one of most abundant pollutants in the atmosphere.

3. Allocation Direct to Global Warming: carbon monoxide was considered in this category because this pollutant is involved in an oxidation process
   generating carbon dioxide, which is a precursor of the greenhouse effect. Nitrogen dioxide (NO2) is a precursor of ozone and acid rain, as well as
   causing the production of particulate matter. Finally, sulphur dioxide (SO2) is a precursor of acid rain and particulate matter.
    Importance parametre levels were consulted and designed by the air quality experts that monitor the pollution atmosphere in the
Mexico Valley area. According to the SIMAT monitoring, weight assignment was deﬁned relating the parametre interaction in the atmo-
sphere. Fig. 7 shows the frequency of the parametre concentrations from January to August 2015 (last actualized database provided by
the SIMAT).
    In this case, PM10 presented the highest concentrations with more harmful occurrences in the period (Fig. 7). This phenomenon is generated by traf-
ﬁc problems that actually is the most problematic pollution source in this place. PM2.5 generates more severe problems in people's health than the
rest of the parametre set; however, it does not represent such a severe problem as PM10 because it rarely overpasses the ﬁrst permissible level.
For this reason it was decided to assign the same highest level to PM2.5 and PM10 (extreme importance). The second criteria was taken according
to the parametre toxicity, where O3 and CO are highly toxic pollutants; nevertheless, O3 weight was deﬁned as very important because it presented
more abundant concentrations than CO in the Mexico Valley area. Although CO is highly toxic, it never overpasses the ﬁrst level, due to this, the cor-
responding weight assigned was moderate importance. Finally NO2 and SO2 were deﬁned as non-critical parametres and the weights assigned were
equal importance in both cases. The previously deﬁned weights have been assigned supported by experts and they can be adjusted according to the
pollution problems necessities.
    In order to determine the weights to be used in the assessment computation, air pollutants are organized by their importance in air pollution. The
Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) has been developed by Thomas L. Saaty (2004) as a way to interpret and transform importance levels into math-
ematical weights that amplify or reduce parametre behaviours useful in a decision making system (Chakraborty and Dey, 2006). In this sense, those
weights used in the FIS affect the result of a rule according to those environmental parametres with higher importance, providing a more sensible
assessment. Therefore, an AHP is an effective tool that provides a priority order to each pollutant and an importance scale that can be consulted in
Table 4.
    The importance of the pollutants depends of the necessities of the area to be monitored. In this work, they were adjusted to the characteristics of
the Mexico City Valley and the recommendations emitted by the Atmospheric Monitoring System of Mexico City (SIMAT, 2014), the Mexican
Ministry of Environment (SEDEMA, 2014) and National Institute of Ecology and Climate Change (INECC, 2014). The next step is to transform
those importance levels in weights (priorities) by making pairwise comparisons. The importance values assigned to each parametre are used to de-
velop a consistent matrix. It is a positive reciprocal n × n matrix whose elements satisfy the relation aij · ajk = aik, for i, j and k = 1,..., n. This pairwise
